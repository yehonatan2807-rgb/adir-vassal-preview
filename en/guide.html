The diamond guide

What is worth knowing before choosing a diamond – briefly, and without the jargon.

By Adir Vassal · Updated November 2026

Diamond shapes

The four Cs

Four qualities determine how a diamond looks and what it costs. None stands alone – the balance between them is what matters.

Cut

Cut

The main source of brilliance. An excellent cut returns light up through the top; a weak one leaks it out of the sides and base. This is not the place to compromise.

Carat

Carat

A carat is a unit of weight – 0.2 grams. Prices jump around 'round' weights, so a 0.9 carat stone can look almost identical to a one-carat stone and cost less.

Before you buy: four things worth knowing

A grading report

A significant diamond usually comes with a report from an independent gemological lab such as GIA or IGI. It lists the stone's qualities, and the report number is often laser-inscribed on the girdle. Ask to see it.

Natural or lab-grown

Lab-grown diamonds are chemically and physically real diamonds, sold at a significantly lower price than natural ones. Both are legitimate choices – what matters is knowing which you are buying, and making sure it is written on the invoice.

Fluorescence

Some diamonds glow a bluish colour under ultraviolet light. In normal light this is almost never visible. Strong fluorescence can occasionally make a stone look slightly milky, while in a stone with a faint yellowish tint it can actually make it look whiter. It is listed on the report, and either way it is worth judging the stone by eye.

How to check a report

Every GIA report has a number. You can enter it on GIA's website, in its Report Check service, and see the stone's original details. That same number is usually laser-inscribed on the diamond's girdle, visible under a loupe.

Ready to choose? All of this is easiest to understand with the stones in front of you.
